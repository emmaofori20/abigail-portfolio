PROJECT 1
UAV Multispectral Remote Sensing and Machine
Learning for Cocoa Nutrient Assessment

MSc research | Juaboso cocoa landscape, Western North, Ghana

Research Context
Cocoa production in Ghana faces challenges associated with declining soil fertility and the limitations of blanket fertilizer
recommendations. My MSc research investigated the use of UAV-based multispectral remote sensing and machine-
learning approaches to assess cocoa foliar nitrogen (N), phosphorus (P), and potassium (K), while also examining
relationships between soil and leaf nutrient concentrations. This research was one component of a broader project,
FRAME-Cocoa Project, sponsored by African Plant Nutrition Institute (APNI).

Research Focus
The thesis had two linked objectives: (1) to quantify relationships between soil and leaf N, P and K concentrations; and
(2) to develop predictive models for cocoa leaf nutrient concentrations using spectral bands and derived vegetation
indices. The study was positioned as a contribution to precision agriculture and spatially informed nutrient assessment.

Research Question
Can UAV multispectral imagery and machine-learning models be used to estimate cocoa leaf nitrogen (N), phosphorus
(P), and potassium (K) concentrations and produce continuous spatial predictions across a cocoa production
landscape?

Data and Remote Sensing Platform
•   DJI Matrice 350 RTK UAV, operated at 150 m altitude and 3 m/s with 65% image overlap.
•   MicaSense RedEdge-P multispectral sensor with Blue (475 nm), Green (560 nm), Red (668 nm), Red Edge (717 nm),
   and NIR (842 nm) bands.
•   Blue, Green, Red, Red Edge and NIR bands and derived vegetation indices.
•   59 composite soil samples/field sampling locations.
•   Ground-truth soil and foliar N, P and K measurements used for model development and evaluation.

Remote Sensing and Data Processing

    •   Imagery was radiometrically corrected using the MicaSense calibration panel and processed in Agisoft
       Metashape to generate a 0.09 m GSD orthomosaic.
    •   Spectral bands and vegetation indices were derived to represent canopy spectral behaviour, chlorophyll-
        related properties and vegetation structure.
    •   Raster predictors were prepared and extracted at the field sampling locations for subsequent statistical and
        machine-learning analysis.
    •   Vegetation-index predictors with inter-correlation greater than 0.95 were excluded to reduce redundancy and
         multicollinearity.
    •   The resulting spectral predictor set was used to compare machine-learning models for foliar nutrient
        estimation.



Machine-Learning Models
•  Random Forest (RF)
•   Support Vector Regression (SVM)
•    Artificial Neural Network (ANN)
•   Partial Least Squares Regression (PLSR)
•   Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost)
•   Gradient Boosting Machine (GBM)

Results and research contribution

Research Output
The modelling results demonstrated that relationships between UAV-derived spectral information and cocoa foliar
nutrients varied by nutrient. Different algorithms therefore provided varying predictive performance for nitrogen,
phosphorus and potassium. Phosphorus and potassium showed stronger predictive signals than nitrogen, while limited
nutrient variability constrained the performance of spectral models. In the soil-to-leaf analysis, Random Forest
produced the strongest fits for Leaf N, Leaf P and Leaf K (R² = 0.17, 0.55 and 0.52, respectively). For spectral-variable
models, performance was generally weak: nitrogen predictions were close to zero R², XGBoost performed best for Leaf
P (R² ≈ 0.09), and ANN had the highest R² for Leaf K (≈ 0.10), although with relatively high errors.

PROJECT 2
Geospatial Characterisation of Shade Canopy in
Cocoa Agroforestry and Soil Resources

Juaboso District, Ghana | Remote-sensing component under FRAME-Cocoa Project

Research Context

This is co-authored research which included field-based and GIS-driven characterisation of shade canopy in cocoa
agroforestry farms within one of Ghana’s prime cocoa landscapes. Shade canopy in cocoa farms can yield its highest
benefits when managed in an optimum range, however, can also be detrimental to cocoa productivity when it falls short
or goes beyond that specific range. This work integrated remote sensing, field observations and mathematical
optimisation.

Data and Field Research

•   Collected soil samples at multiple depths from cocoa farms across Juaboso District.
•   Identified and measured canopy sizes of shade trees present within sampled farms.
•  Conducted and supported laboratory analysis of soil properties.
•   Characterised shade-tree cover and cocoa agroforestry structure.

My Remote-Sensing Contribution
In addition to the field data collection, I worked on the use of Sentinel-2-derived information to characterise cocoa farms
according to shade level. The remote-sensing workflow used spectral, texture, and biophysical predictors and Random
Forest modelling.

Remote-Sensing Predictors
•   Sentinel-2 imagery covering dry season observations.
•   Spectral variables from Sentinel bands and vegetation indices.
•   Texture variables derived using GLCM metrics.
•   Biophysical variables including LAI, fAPAR, fCover, chlorophyll content and canopy water content.

Classification Approach
Random Forest modelling was used to predict continuous canopy-cover information, which was subsequently
categorised into four shade levels; no shade, low shade, moderate shade (optimal), and high shade.

PROJECT 4
Land-Use/Land-Cover Classification in Ghana's High
Forest Zone

Remote sensing, GIS and reference-data based classification

Project Context
This work originated as part of broader research led by my supervisor on forest and land-cover mapping in Ghana's High
Forest Zone. Under this research, we sought to investigate which algorithm best distinguishes land use classes in a highly
heterogenous landscape such as the high forest zone of Ghana.

My Contribution
•   Executed the assigned remote-sensing and GIS component.
•   Prepared and analysed satellite imagery for land-cover classification.
•  Worked with training/reference data for supervised classification.
•   Produced and interpreted LULC classification outputs.
•   Applied geospatial analysis to support the broader forest/landscape research.

Technical Context
The High Forest Zone research addresses forest mapping in a spectrally heterogeneous landscape and considers
medium-resolution Landsat and Sentinel Earth observation datasets, including comparison of traditional machine
learning classifiers and CNN-based deep learning.

LULC Workflow
•   Satellite imagery preparation and preprocessing.
•   Preparation/use of training and reference data.
•   Supervised land-cover classification.
•   Accuracy assessment and spatial interpretation.
